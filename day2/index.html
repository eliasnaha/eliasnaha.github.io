The machine designs its own games

Yesterday the harness filled in the numbers of scenes we had designed. Today the model writes the whole game from one prompt: its entities, its own action set, its rules, its win and lose conditions — and its milestones, the checkpoints the verifier uses to tell it exactly where a broken game is stuck.

games3 originals, 3 mechanics attempts8 total failures caught5, all repaired verifiercode-only, no VLM

The harness got out of the game-design business. The generator prompt no longer contains a worked example or a genre — only a minimal physics vocabulary (bodies, joints, sensors, forces) and a contract: declare your actions, write their semantics, define success, failure, and 1–6 ordered milestone predicates that decompose your own rules.

What the game can never define is sanity. Universal oracles run outside its reach: numerical health, determinism, agency (doing nothing must never win), action efficacy (every declared action must measurably do something), goal well-formedness, and solvability — a seeded random search that must actually win the game. The winning action sequence it finds — the witness — is what plays in the clips below.

Every failed attempt below was caught by a machine check and repaired through the report alone: no human looked at the game code. The stuck-between-milestones diagnosis did the pinpointing.

What the verifier caught

Attempt 1 rejected: coast was a dead action — measured world-effect exactly 0.0 while burn scored 91. The repair made every action live. The game's own twist: free-fall is always lethal, so winning demands timed retro burns.

02 wrecking_ball_yard pass
>a wrecking ball on a chain: swing it to knock the crate tower off its ledge

What the verifier caught

Attempt 1 unsolvable — diagnosis: stuck between swing_started and reached_crates, and the action-efficacy numbers were lopsided (80 vs 206): the ledge itself was blocking the swing path. One geometry fix, then a pendulum you pump into resonance — no single action wins.

03 volcano_hopper pass
>volcano hopper: climb the stone slabs before the rising lava catches you

What the verifier caught

Three rejections: a dead wait action, then twice stuck between the first and second slab. The diagnosis localized the broken hop; the fix was a redesign — a diamond with two winning paths and a lava clock that makes hesitation fatal.

* amber flashes in the clips are milestones latching in real time — the game's own checkpoints, recorded by the harness the first time each becomes true. They double as the dense, code-defined progress signal a reward model could train on.

How a generated game is checked

universal oracles · the game can't redefine them
G0 · static

Does it build?

Sandbox-clean, required symbols, exactly one controlled body, nothing overlapping or out of bounds.

G1 · rollout

Is it sane & honest?

No NaNs or escapes; deterministic; doing nothing never wins; every declared action measurably does something.

G2 · goal

Are goal & milestones real?

Pure predicates, false at t=0; 1–6 ordered milestones that decompose the game's own rules.

G3 · solvability

Can it be won?

Seeded random search must win it; milestones guide a second pass and pinpoint where a failing game is stuck.
